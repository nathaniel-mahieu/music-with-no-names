# Model cards and version registry

These cards describe educational models used by Music With No Names. A model output is evidence under stated assumptions, never a verdict about musical goodness.

## Ratio and waveform geometry

- **Version:** `mwno-math-1` (source-controlled pure functions).
- **Inputs:** positive frequencies, continuous ratios, seconds, phase, partial count.
- **Outputs:** sine samples, normalized sums, rational approximations, common periods, harmonic partials, partial coincidences, prime coordinates, and log-frequency distances.
- **Validation:** closed-form waveform, spectrum, ratio-period, and voice-motion fixtures.
- **Limits:** rational approximation depends on denominator and cent tolerances; a shared physical period is not a perceptual or musical center.

## Ear Lab auditory models

- **Version:** `mwno-ear-2`.
- **Inputs:** explicit generated partial spectra, register, spacing, inharmonicity, noise, level control, attack, and duration; plus the latest two valid tab-scoped Piano attacks for the live interval bridge.
- **Outputs:** Plomp–Levelt/Sethares-style roughness, critical-band-rate energy projection, spectral overlap, harmonic-template candidates, and a declared fusion hypothesis. The live bridge reports measured MIDI key spacing, onset spacing, and release-proven overlap alongside the equal-tempered ratio and A4=440 reference frequencies derived from those key numbers, then compares sine, exact-harmonic, mellow-piano proxy, and bright-piano proxy spectra only for a proven simultaneous interval.
- **Fusion formula:** 42% spectral overlap + 45% best template confidence + 13% inverse roughness. It is deliberately simple and visually separated from the listener's fusion and liking reports.
- **Confidence:** template confidence is relative fit inside the searched candidate set. It is not a calibrated probability that the listener hears one pitch.
- **Live interval boundary:** MIDI supplies key numbers and event timing, not acoustic pitch, upper partials, or level. The bridge derives 12-TET reference frequencies at A4=440 and does not read pitch bend or instrument/DAW tuning. A sequential pair or missing release time receives no simultaneous spectral-interaction output. Model partial counts, alignments, roughness, harmonicity, fusion, and brightness are teaching assumptions; they do not measure the connected keyboard patch, DAW, speaker, room, or ear.
- **Limits:** no individualized auditory filters, calibrated SPL, masking model, binaural model, hearing-profile correction, listener-state estimate, emotion inference, or goodness score.

## Harmony affordance model

- **Version:** `mwno-sonority-2`.
- **Inputs:** absolute frequency, amplitude, partial count, normalized ratio field, and recent voice-leading distance.
- **Auditory outputs:** aggregate roughness, strongest harmonic-template fit, competing-template ambiguity, pairwise spectral overlap, amplitude-weighted spectral height, fusion, and outer-voice spread.
- **Structural outputs:** repose combines 42% harmonic fit, 33% inverse roughness, and 25% low recent motion. Tension combines 42% roughness, 24% competing-center ambiguity, 22% recent motion, and 12% spectral brightness.
- **Affordances:** repose/groundedness, friction/activation, openness/spaciousness, and brightness/lift are kept as separate dimensions. Each is phrased as something a sound *may support*, never an emotion it contains.
- **Live chord-change bridge:** the latest two valid Piano gestures are grouped with the learner's declared 80, 160, or 320 ms timing and explicit break/join corrections. Each field must contain at least two attacks and two pitch classes. Bass-relative equal-key offsets and nearest-key voice motion are measured from MIDI positions; pairwise 12-TET ratios are derived from those offsets. The current sonority is then recomputed under one fixed nine-partial exact-harmonic stack rolling off at 7 dB/octave, with motion supplied separately. Four signed affordance deltas remain conditional invitations. MIDI velocity is intentionally excluded because it is not calibrated acoustic level.
- **Confidence:** values are bounded educational indices with visible drivers. They are not probabilities, preference estimates, emotion classifiers, or a consonance score.
- **Limits:** no familiarity corpus, individualized hearing model, temporal expectation beyond one previous field, tempo, dynamics, articulation, spatialization, semantic content, culture, or personal association. The live bridge receives attacked events, not a time-resolved audio signal, and does not reconstruct notes inherited through holding or pedal at each boundary; actual timbre, balance, room, tonal function, and listener experience remain unknown. Formula weights are explanatory choices, not fitted coefficients.

- **Inputs:** up to sixty seconds of local note-on events (bounded to 256 attacks), velocity, key release, sounding release, pedal extension, simultaneous held/pedaled field, optional chord-boundary corrections, optional per-gesture excluded inherited MIDI notes, an optional locked movable Do, the current scale frame, optional frozen A/B phrase specimens and three comparative listener reports, optional guided-walk start event and fixed route, an optional frozen tonal-gravity specimen/target/cue, an optional exact-register controlled-sonority baseline, an optional four-tap pulse source and Motion question, a locally selected ghost target or generated landmark path, path-step progress, an optional generated-route A/B session containing only path IDs and A's structural variant, and optional conventional-label visibility. Coordinated detail views inspect the latest seven attacks.
- **Relational outputs:** A4=440 12-TET reference frequency in hertz derived from MIDI key number, equal-key ratio and cents from Do, movable syllable, selected-scale membership, pairwise interval landmarks, and repeated-fifths coordinates. Pitch bend, instrument tuning, and acoustic pitch are not read.
- **Fifths derivation:** each pure move multiplies frequency by 3:2 and removes octaves until the result lies within one octave. The Scales focus renders thirteen cumulative positions, including the attempted return. A declared interpolation changes each fifth from `1200 × log2(3/2) = 701.955…` cents toward the equal-key 700 cents; accumulated closure error therefore moves continuously from approximately 23.46 cents to zero. Diagram angle carries the accumulated cents beyond the equal-key position, while radius is only a visual separation of successive moves. Selecting a compass or non-return spiral position locks that absolute pitch class as movable Do without creating a MIDI event; the scale fingerprint remains unchanged under this transposition.
- **Scale candidates:** all twelve possible homes are tested against four declared pitch-class routes. Fit combines 72% observed-note membership, 18% route coverage, and 10% candidate-home presence. It is a compatibility ranking, not key detection certainty.
- **Performed tonal gravity:** each possible center is paired with its best-fitting declared route, then ranked by 42% route membership, 17% capped logarithmic sounding duration, 14% recurrence, 9% MIDI attack strength, 8% low-register presence, and 10% presence among the last three attacks. Pedal extension contributes 35% of its capped duration, and older attacks receive a bounded recency discount. Components are normalized within the local phrase and shown separately. This is an inspectable teaching heuristic, not a detected key, probability, listener model, or population fit.
- **Tonal-gravity counterfactual:** opening the microscope freezes three to twenty-four recent attacks, normalizes their timestamps relative to the first attack, and fixes the current movable-Do route. The learner chooses one observed pitch class and one of the five performed-evidence lanes. That lane becomes `1` for the target center and `0` for the other eleven candidates; route fit and the remaining four component values are copied exactly from the frozen baseline. The same declared weights produce a paired twelve-center ranking and an exact target rank delta. This is a one-variable sensitivity intervention inside the heuristic, not a synthesized performance, causal claim about hearing, key detection, confidence, tension, preference, or quality estimate.
- **Intention-led five-lens phrase A/B:** phrase A is the latest three-to-twelve attacks; when a pause longer than 1.6 seconds leaves at least three newer attacks, that suffix becomes the specimen. Before capture, the learner declares transposition, timing, MIDI touch, articulation, one interval, or the ending as the coordinate to investigate. The capture freezes open releases, normalizes timestamps to the first attack, locks the movable-Do frame, and starts phrase B after an explicit event ID. Target detection uses the same disclosed display thresholds as the five lanes. Each intention also has a control: uniform signed-interval preservation for transposition; identical absolute pitch paths for timing, touch, or articulation; exactly one changed signed move for the interval experiment; or an unchanged earlier path plus one changed final move for the ending experiment. The physical lane compares mean MIDI key, key span, and mean velocity. The relationship lane compares signed semitone paths. The motion lane compares normalized onset spacing, elapsed-time ratio, and sounding overlap. Context independently reranks tonal-gravity hypotheses and reports ending position and model separation. Experience contains only learner reports. The interface names target, control, other changed lenses, and invariants independently; it does not create execution, isolation, similarity, causal, musicality, or goodness scores.
- **Scale fingerprint and resolution forks:** the selected route is rendered as a cyclic string of one-, two-, or three-key gaps totaling twelve. Rotation changes only the displayed starting point and does not change the pitch set. Resolution forks are unranked invitations: return to the selected center, minimize one keyboard move, visit the selected center’s fifths neighbor, or choose a less-recent in-route pitch class. Selecting a fork freezes the choices and marks a pitch class silently; only a later learner attack can match it.
- **Guided ascending scale walk:** starting the exercise locks the current movable-Do pitch class and declared route without creating an attack. The first later attack matching Do establishes the MIDI register in any octave. Each following attack must equal the prior matched route position plus the declared one-, two-, or three-key gap; a mismatch increments an error count but preserves all matched positions and exposes attempted versus expected motion. Playing any Do during an active route restarts from that MIDI note. The upper Do completes only at twelve equal steps, where its 12-TET reference frequency is exactly twice the base reference. The interface marks one exact silent keyboard target when it falls inside the visible range and relates it to its equal-tempered frequency ratio, nearby small-integer interval landmark, repeated-fifths coordinate, and separately computed performed tonal-gravity evidence.
- **Performed scale fingerprint:** the first post-arm attack defines an arbitrary MIDI origin. Later accepted attacks must rise strictly and remain no more than twelve equal key steps above it. Reaching exactly twelve closes the authored loop; successive accepted-note differences become the ordered fingerprint. In replay mode, the first attack may again be anywhere, but only the next declared gap advances. Descending moves, octave overshoots, and wrong replay gaps increment visible error evidence while preserving the accepted prefix. Rotation cyclically moves the first gap to the end and is distinguished from transposition. Exact catalog comparison occurs only after octave closure and is shown only on learner request; no match is treated as a catalog limit, not a validity or quality judgment.
- **Scale Landing Mutation:** a completed performed fingerprint can become a frozen source while the learner authors another strictly ascending twelve-step route from any MIDI origin. Cumulative positions begin at zero and end at twelve. The comparator returns `one-position` only when source and attempt contain the same number of gaps, exactly one source-only internal position, exactly one attempt-only internal position, every other position is retained, and the replacement differs by exactly one equal-key step. Elementwise gap deltas expose how that minimal move redistributes space across neighboring gaps while both routes keep octave closure. An unchanged route, a wider one-position move, a changed gap count, and multiple moved positions are separate refusal states. The comparison does not infer a scale name, movable Do, note spelling, tonal center, function, intonation outside twelve-equal temperament, acoustics, emotion, correctness, preference, quality, or learning gain.
- **Scale Interval Ripple:** after an exact one-key landing mutation, the changed landing is paired separately with every retained normalized route position, including zero and twelve as distinct physical octave endpoints. Each spoke reports absolute equal-key distance, `2^(distance/12)` as the ascending 12-TET frequency multiplier, and the signed one-step distance change. Spokes that touch the moved landing are counted as changed; all unordered pairs among retained positions are counted as invariant controls. If `n` positions remain, the control count is `n(n−1)/2`. The display does not compare absolute replay frequencies, which may change with the MIDI origin, and it does not model tuning outside 12-TET, spectra, consonance, tonal function, emotion, preference, quality, or learning gain.
- **Controlled sonority fields:** four authored starting recipes use exact equal-key offsets `[0,4,7]`, `[0,3,7]`, `[0,5,7]`, and `[0,1,2]`; a learner may instead capture any currently active field of at least two MIDI notes. A recipe outlines exact keys without adding an attack or producing sound. Once the learner performs the baseline, a set difference permits causal output only when exactly one MIDI note entered or left. The physical lane reports only intervals connecting that changed note to retained notes plus the outer-span delta. The auditory lane recomputes roughness, fusion, harmonicity, and brightness under the declared teaching spectrum. The contextual lane recomputes selected-route membership and pull toward the locked Do. Four conditional `mwno-sonority-2` affordances remain independent and are invitations to listen, not emotion or quality estimates. Multiple changes suppress all causal lanes. Because active keys are not persisted, reload marks a stored baseline as requiring exact replay; missing keys stay silently outlined and partial replay cannot masquerade as a one-note removal.
- **Motion question focus and live pulse:** the Motion focus renders exactly one of Pulse, Touch, Voices, or Motif while retaining the same phrase. Pulse begins only after the learner explicitly arms it. The first subsequent MIDI note fixes an anchor key; the first four attacks of that exact key define three gaps. Every gap must remain between 180 ms and 2 seconds. Their median is the displayed pulse duration and their mean absolute deviation from that median is the raw anchor spread. Later attacks beginning within 70 ms of the cluster's first attack share one onset, preventing chord density from becoming false rhythmic speed. Each cluster is placed by phase modulo the declared pulse and compared with the nearest 0, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, or 3/4 phase coordinate. Consecutive cluster gaps are divided by the pulse and compared logarithmically with 1:4, 1:3, 1:2, 2:3, 3:4, 1:1, 3:2, 2:1, 3:1, or 4:1 landmarks. These are timing coordinates under a learner-supplied pulse, not detected meter, notation, accent, swing, groove, accuracy, or quality.
- **Articulation evidence:** finger contact ends at key release; pedal extension is sounding release minus key release; silence or overlap is measured against the next attack. A release is treated as edge-connected inside a tolerance bounded from 25 to 80 ms and otherwise set to 10% of the local inter-onset interval. The lane distinguishes still held, phrase ending, detached, connected, finger-overlapped, and pedal-linked timing. These are captured-event descriptions, not inferred note values, legato markings, fingering, or technique assessment.
- **Motif transformations:** non-overlapping windows of three or four note attacks are represented by exact semitone offsets from the first attack and onset gaps normalized by total window duration. Identical pitches and close rhythm form an exact repeat; constant-shift pitch shape and close rhythm form a transposed repeat; the same pitch shape with a normalized rhythm distance from 0.12 through 0.5 is a rhythmic variation; and a matching three-note opening with a changed fourth note is an altered ending. A later match separated by other attacks is labeled as a return after intervening material. The detector is phrase-local and deterministic, not a learned form or salience model.
- **Playable landmark paths:** four authored harmonic archetypes—four-field pop return, seventh-colored blues turn, ii–V7–I cadence, and fixed-Do pedal field—are stored as pitch-class offsets from movable Do, so transposition preserves the entire relationship sequence. Entering Paths locks the current Do. Each step is voiced once near the previous step by minimizing symmetric nearest-key distance, then remains fixed while the learner enters it. A step advances only when the live field contains exactly the target pitch classes; octave and inversion are free. Transition evidence reports carried pitch classes, minimum mapped keyboard motion, largest leap, and root distance around fifths separately from the standardized crunch, repose, and tonal-tendency proxies. The examples are generated archetypes with explicit provenance, not transcriptions, recordings, style classifiers, or claims about every piece in a genre.
- **Personal Character Map:** entering the Experience focus freezes the latest live phrase for reflection rather than allowing later attacks to rewrite the specimen. The learner reports settledness, energy, surprise/familiarity, and liking on four separate five-position questions. The first two dimensions place a point; reported liking controls point size and reported familiarity controls ring weight. The map shows arithmetic centers, population spread, and a deliberately conservative uncertainty radius of `max(5, 30 / sqrt(sample count))`. A relationship signature uses successive semitone intervals plus onset gaps normalized by phrase duration and bucketed to twenty-four steps, making the comparison invariant to transposition and uniform tempo scaling. Matching signatures are a structural comparison aid, not evidence that two performances, contexts, or experiences are the same.
- **Experience evidence lanes:** the reflected specimen preserves measured attack count, elapsed phrase time, pitch span, mean MIDI velocity, and sounding overlap; modeled crunch, ending repose, pitch novelty, and center clarity; and the learner's four reports in three visibly separate lanes. No formula combines them, and no measured or modeled value is used to infer the reported axes.
- **Tonal tendency outputs:** pull toward selected Do weights chromatic neighbors and the fifth relation, then reduces unresolved pull when Do is already present. Home evidence combines Do presence, fifth support, and selected-route membership. Repose/arrival evidence combines 55% `mwno-sonority-2` repose with 45% home evidence; change is compared only with the previous held field.
- **Selectable spectral outputs:** the HUD offers four declared teaching spectra: a one-partial sine; a nine-partial exact harmonic stack rolling off at 7 dB/octave; a twelve-partial mellow-piano proxy rolling off at 10 dB/octave with inharmonicity `0.00008` and noise amount `0.01`; and a sixteen-partial bright-piano proxy rolling off at 5 dB/octave with inharmonicity `0.00016` and noise amount `0.025`. String-stretch multiples are normalized so the first partial stays at the app's A4=440 12-TET reference frequency while upper partials rise slightly. The selected model changes harmonic-template fit, modeled spectral crunch, brightness, and the spectral share of repose. It does not change MIDI keys, reference coordinates, intervals, scale/fifths placement, rhythm, tonal pull toward Do, pitch novelty, or listener reports. MIDI contains no audio, so none of these profiles measures the actual pitch or spectrum of the connected keyboard, patch, DAW, room, or listener.
- **Live partial-interaction microscope:** when at least two notes sound, the focused event is paired with its nearest sounding neighbor; without a focused sounding event, the two-note field remains directly inspectable. Each fundamental is expanded through the selected assumed teaching spectrum, noise components are omitted from the comb and pair-link plot, and partial height is placed on one logarithmic frequency axis. Cross-partial pairs within 18 cents are labeled as aligned. Non-aligned pairs whose normalized interaction is at least `0.035` are ranked by amplitude-weighted pair roughness and the strongest six are shown as interaction-zone links. The displayed roughness index still aggregates every cross-partial contribution, including weak unlinked pairs, while spectral overlap is computed and displayed separately. These thresholds and truncation are legibility conventions, not perceptual boundaries. The microscope does not analyze instrument or DAW audio and does not estimate consonance, fusion, emotion, preference, listenability, or musical quality.
- **Interval Context Echo:** arming Echo freezes the two ordered MIDI events and their unsigned equal-key distance. Later attacks are partitioned from the anchor into non-overlapping consecutive pairs, so the second note of one attempt cannot silently become the first note of the next. A match requires equal unsigned distance. Five lenses then keep the invariant equal-tempered ratio separate from A4=440 reference frequencies and hertz gap, direction and attack spacing, the number of MIDI notes sounding at the second attack, current movable-Do labels, and unclaimed listener experience. A source or echo is simultaneous only when the first event's release time is later than the second attack; missing release remains unknown. Selected-spectrum roughness and partial overlap are compared only when both performed pairs prove overlap. Restored phrases may use a negative relative-time origin, but their ordering and release-after-attack constraints remain required. The exercise does not infer fingering, intonation, acoustic pitch or loudness, actual instrument audio, tonal function, perceptual identity, correctness, emotion, or quality.
- **Interval Experience Handoff:** after an exact echo match, the learner may freeze the two source events and two replay events as one ordered local reflection specimen. The handoff does not replace or shorten the sixty-second live phrase. The Personal Character Map derives its measured and assumed-spectrum rows from those four events, while settledness, energy, familiarity, and liking remain unanswered until the listener explicitly reports them. The relationship signature supports later within-listener comparison but is not a claim that transposed, retimed, or differently voiced experiences are perceptually identical. Reports remain browser-local and join the existing Personal Lens export and deletion flow.
- **Chord Change Experience Handoff:** Chords focus selects the latest corrected gesture and its predecessor, then presents five separate readings. Sound combines measured field size and key span with a visibly declared spectrum-dependent roughness delta; Relationships reports entered, left, and retained interpreted pitch-class positions; Motion uses one nearest-key parsimonious mapping without claiming intended voices or fingering; Context exposes selected-Do pull, repose, and exact-root fifths travel as teaching coordinates; Experience remains unanswered. Selecting reflection copies the exact attacks from both gestures into one ordered local specimen and leaves the sixty-second phrase intact. That bounded comparison persists across focus changes until the learner explicitly chooses the whole live phrase, and Clear returns the origin safely to phrase mode. Settledness, energy, familiarity, and liking enter the model only through listener answers; no lane becomes correctness, consonance, emotion, listenability, or musical goodness.
- **Chord Voicing Echo:** the learner chooses the New voicing question and freezes one corrected grouped field containing two through six MIDI positions and at least two pitch classes. The source retains its exact grouped attacks plus any earlier attacks that supplied interpreted held or pedaled members. A later independent gesture matches when its unique pitch-class set is identical to the source or is the result of one uniform modulo-twelve transposition. Register, inversion, bass role, span, doubling, physical center, nearest-key strands, assumed-spectrum roughness, and selected-Do tendency remain separate outputs rather than match criteria. A mismatch leaves the source fixed for another attempt. A match may copy the performed source and latest revoicing events into a bounded Personal Character Map specimen; the listener still supplies every experiential answer. The model does not infer intended fingering or voices, acoustic sound, perceptual identity, chord function, emotion, correctness, preference, listenability, or goodness.
- **Octave Fold:** a matched Chord Voicing Echo may project each field to unique modulo-twelve positions, compute every positive adjacent gap including closure back to the first position, and select the lexicographically smallest cyclic rotation as a common display anchor. Register, voice order, bass position, spelling, and octave doubling are deliberately removed. The output exposes an interval-class-set invariant under inversion of physical voicing and uniform transposition; for two pitch classes it also identifies complementary interval directions. The canonical start does not estimate root, Do, tonal function, intended voice leading, spectrum, consonance, emotional character, correctness, preference, or goodness.
- **Gap Mutation:** when an unmatched Chord Voicing Echo has equal unique-position counts, one source-only position, one attempt-only position, and at least two retained positions, the model begins both closed gap loops at one retained comparison anchor. It returns source and attempt gap arrays, their signed elementwise deltas, the replaced positions, and the shortest signed modulo-twelve displacement. The display can therefore attribute the changed loop to one set-position replacement without claiming which physical voice was intended to move. It refuses two-note fields, additions, removals, multi-position changes, and existing structural matches. The anchor and displacement do not estimate root, movable Do, spelling, voice identity, function, acoustic causality, consonance, emotion, correctness, preference, or goodness.
- **Chord Motion Echo:** after the learner inspects one corrected two-chord change, an explicit action freezes both fields and their supplying attacks. Each field contains two through six MIDI positions and at least two pitch classes. A replay is two new valid grouped fields after an explicit attempt anchor. The complete move matches only when one modulo-twelve shift maps the source-before set to replay-before and the source-after set to replay-after; inversion, register, and pitch-class doubling do not enter that rule. The model also exposes the endpoint shifts independently, so two individually preserved chord types with inconsistent shifts are not mislabeled as one preserved transformation. Common, entered, and left pitch-class counts remain inspectable, while physical voice mapping, bass motion, spans, assumed-spectrum paths, selected-Do tendency, and listener response are computed separately. A retry advances the attempt anchor without replacing the source. Only a structural match can create a bounded four-field listener specimen. The result is not intended voice leading, harmonic function, acoustic or perceptual identity, emotional direction, correctness, preference, musicality, or goodness.
- **Tonal Frame Echo:** after a Chord Motion Echo matches under a nonzero shift, the learner may keep replay Do fixed or move it by that exact shift. The movable option changes the replay reference pitch class while preserving the current scale's twelve-key gap route, then recomputes relative syllables, pull toward Do, and home evidence. Under uniform equal-key transposition these selected-context outputs are invariant by construction; the fixed-Do view is the control. MIDI attacks, derived reference frequencies, register, spacing, assumed spectra, physical voice paths, structural matching, and the listener specimen are not recomputed. Exact restoration is evidence about this declared coordinate model, not proof of equivalent tonal hearing or function.
- **Selected-attack change reading:** Explore compares the selected event's captured field snapshot with the immediately preceding event inside the seven-attack microscope. It delegates set attribution to the same `controlledSonorityChange` rule as the controlled chord experiment. Pairwise relationships are attributed to the selected attack only when exactly one MIDI note was added, no note was removed, and the added note equals the selected attack. A repeated attack is labeled as unchanged membership; a one-note removal, simultaneous addition and removal, or any larger set difference is shown as snapshot change without a one-note cause; the earliest visible event has no invented empty baseline. Sound reports MIDI key number, its derived A4=440 reference frequency, attack value, and field size. Relationships report newly created pairs or the signed melodic move. Motion reports signed key movement, onset spacing, and captured sounding duration. Context reports before/after pull and home evidence only when a prior snapshot exists. Experience remains explicitly unclaimed and links to the learner-report workflow. These five lanes are not averaged and do not infer acoustic pitch or loudness, fingering, technique, emotion, correctness, listenability, or quality.
- **Landmark path transposition:** completing a generated route enables an explicit move from the current Do to its clockwise fifths neighbor. Every target pitch class is translated by the same seven-step equal-key offset while the authored field order, roles, root offsets, and within-field interval offsets are held constant. The route restarts at field one and requires fresh performed pitch-class matches. Choosing another Do directly from the fifths compass uses the same progress reset and records the before/after center. This is a controlled relationship comparison, not evidence that register, voicing, felt direction, style identity, listener response, correctness, musicality, or quality stayed the same.
- **One-tone landmark counterfactual:** each generated route declares one field, one source pitch offset, and one replacement pitch offset. The source path is voiced first; the intervention changes only the matching MIDI key in the declared field by the shortest signed pitch-class move. Every other MIDI target in the full route is copied exactly, so this is a strict one-key physical control rather than a revoiced chord comparison. The learner must replay every field. The altered field reports retained notes, source-versus-target distances from the changed tone, selected-spectrum roughness and repose deltas, and selected-Do pull and home-evidence deltas. A separate optional report asks which complete path felt more directed. The feature does not infer that the authored change caused the report or predicts emotion, style, goodness, correctness, preference, or learning.
- **Fifths limits:** the continuous pure-to-equal interpolation is an explanatory morph, not a catalog of historical temperaments or a simulation of piano tuning. Real tuning practice can distribute comma adjustments nonuniformly, stretch octaves, and depend on instrument, repertoire, and culture. The diagram does not claim that a fifths path determines harmonic function, key, emotion, or quality.
- **MIDI behavior and privacy:** permission and messages stay in the browser. Note-on, note-off, velocity, and sustain drive only the live local visualization; Piano Lab never synthesizes, records, or routes audio. The bounded sixty-second phrase, A/B frozen specimens/replay anchor/declared change/comparative reports, grouping corrections, inherited-membership corrections, guided-walk frame/progress anchor, performed-fingerprint source and replay route, tonal-gravity frozen specimen/target/cue/frame, controlled-sonority recipe/baseline, selected Motion question, four-tap pulse source identity, and local learning settings use tab-scoped session storage so they survive lab navigation and disappear when that tab session ends. If any stored source tap leaves the bounded phrase, the pulse is marked expired rather than being silently rebuilt from later notes. A locked movable Do, scale identifier, and selected Motion question are encoded as `pianoDo`, `pianoScale`, and `pianoMotion` URL parameters; these are learning coordinates, not performed notes. Explicit Personal Character reports use local browser storage so they can reappear in the Personal Lens and join export or two-step deletion; nothing is uploaded.
- **Frame and chord limits:** an automatic frame waits for at least four distinct pitch classes, uses the sixty-second phrase rather than only the seven-note microscope, and still reports alternatives; this is catalog compatibility, not key detection. Chords are pitch-class template matches and cannot infer function, voicing intention, style, or emotional meaning. Incomplete matches are labeled as outlines and do not receive an exact root. Nearby chords first minimize pitch-class changes inside the displayed scale, then choose a compact realization near the current hand position; neither stage predicts what should come next.
- **Temporal grouping and membership:** note attacks join a chord gesture when each adjacent gap fits the visible 80, 160, or 320 ms setting and the whole gesture remains within twice that duration. A learner may explicitly break before or join a selected attack; the correction is displayed and reversible. At least two attacks and two distinct pitch classes are required. Notes already held or sustained at the gesture’s close are labeled as inherited rather than attacked. They begin inside the interpreted membership and can be excluded or restored per MIDI note. This correction changes a declared harmonic reading, never the recorded attack history or physically sounding field.
- **Chord-transition outputs:** pitch-set novelty is Jaccard distance between successive interpreted pitch-class sets; voice motion is a symmetric nearest-voice keyboard-distance proxy normalized to one octave; root travel is reported only between exact interpreted template matches and measures shortest distance around the fifths cycle. Tonal tendency, chord identity, causal membership, nearby targets, and voice strands use the same interpreted membership. Modeled roughness and the interval texture retain every sounding note, including excluded inherited notes, so a contextual correction cannot erase acoustic evidence.
- **Voice-leading and targets:** completed interpreted chord memberships are paired by the minimum total absolute keyboard distance among currently represented voices. Strands report held, up, down, added, and released voices; motion classes report parallel, contrary, oblique, or changing voice count when those patterns are present. This is a nearest-key mapping, not inferred fingering, voice identity, or compositional intent. Nearby-chord choices create visible ghost keys only; the learner must supply the MIDI or on-screen attacks before a pitch-class match is reported.
- **Limits:** equal temperament is the keyboard coordinate system, not a claim of natural superiority. Velocity is not calibrated loudness. Scale membership is not correctness. The guided walk recognizes one exact ascending equal-tempered route and does not assess fingering, fluency, descending motion, rhythm, expressive timing, intonation, or whether a scale choice is musically appropriate. The A/B phrase boundary is learner-declared with only a transparent recent-pause convenience; the comparator does not align inserted or omitted attacks, infer which change was intended, measure actual audio, explain a listener's response, or establish that one visible delta caused another. Controlled sonority fields isolate set membership under an exact-register teaching model; real auditory results also depend on the connected instrument, voicing balance, room, hearing, timing, articulation, and broader sequence, none of which MIDI note data measures. The live pulse mirror assumes one stationary pulse from four same-key taps; expressive tempo change, intended subdivisions, polyrhythm, swing, groove, metric hierarchy, accompaniment, latency outside captured browser timestamps, and the learner's bodily entrainment remain unmodeled. The compatibility frame ignores note order and duration; the separate tonal-gravity heuristic uses local duration and order but is not trained or listener-validated. Its counterfactual assigns one complete cue lane rather than generating a physically plausible new performance, so it exposes only formula sensitivity and cannot estimate listener response or interaction among cues. Tonal pull assumes the selected Do and declared scale frame. Articulation labels depend on MIDI timing and do not recover score notation. Motif matching ignores harmony, meter, accent hierarchy, phrase boundaries, orchestration, style, and listener salience. Landmark paths reduce broad musical practices to short harmonic teaching archetypes and omit melody, meter, groove, articulation, production, historical context, and listener familiarity; their presence does not identify a style or predict emotion. Personal Character reports are sparse self-reports, not stable traits; their axes can interact, depend on wording and context, and do not establish why an experience occurred. Matching signatures ignore register, voicing, dynamics, articulation detail, timbre, pedal, meter, and broader context. Resolution forks do not predict the intended, stylistic, emotionally effective, or correct continuation. Conditional language such as “may support” is not an emotion classifier or listener prediction.

## Rhythm and pulse models

- **Version:** `mwno-rhythm-3`.
- **Inputs:** circular onset positions, tempo, microtiming, optional listener taps, optional live MIDI attacks following an explicitly armed four-tap same-key pulse declaration, and a bounded tab-scoped phrase containing attack, velocity, and optional release times.
- **Outputs:** ranked pulse hypotheses, nested phase, transparent syncopation, estimated tap tempo, tap consistency, raw declared-pulse spread, clustered attack phase, inter-onset gap ratios, and a pitchless phrase profile. The phrase profile clusters attacks within 70 ms, takes the median inter-cluster gap as a local unit, reports each exact multiple and nearest simple ratio landmark, marks recurrence only when normalized gap lengths lie within 0.12 octaves, and distinguishes known overlap, edge connection, silence, and missing release evidence.
- **Confidence:** pulse confidence is normalized pattern support among the declared candidate divisions.
- **Limits:** the live mirror uses a fixed pulse, simple phase and gap landmarks, and a declared 70 ms onset-cluster window. The pitchless bridge's median unit is not a detected pulse, and a nearest ratio is a coordinate rather than an intended notation value. MIDI velocity is not acoustic loudness. Release evidence depends on the input and cannot recover written articulation or pedaling intention. Neither model establishes meter, beat hierarchy, notation, swing, groove, timing accuracy, entrainment, expressive tempo, bodily response, or musical quality for a listener.

## Journey prediction models

- **Version:** `mwno-prediction-2`.
- **Live phrase trail:** attacks beginning within 70 ms of a group's first onset form one field event. Each field is represented by pitch offsets from its bass, signed bass motion from the preceding field, and onset spacing divided by the phrase's median inter-group gap. The exact field-shape-plus-bass-motion gesture is transposition invariant; proportional tempo scaling preserves its timing profile.
- **Piece-local model:** transition counts are learned incrementally only from earlier events in the current generated or live sequence.
- **Synthetic-corpus model:** a small declared gesture-transition prior used for teaching model dependence. It is not trained on, or representative of, a musical culture.
- **Listener-personalized model:** transition counts begin with next-gesture expectations the listener explicitly teaches and stores locally, then update with the current piece. With no observations it reduces to the piece-local model.
- **Outputs:** alternatives, uncertainty before an event, surprise after an event, and a live event-by-event trail that distinguishes opening, a gesture with no earlier continuation, a new continuation outside prior evidence, and a continuation already supported by the phrase.
- **Limits:** generated-journey gestures are curated labels; live gestures are deliberately coarse MIDI relationships. Exact recurrence ignores register while field shape retains voicing above the bass. Timing is displayed but does not determine the continuation key. Transition counts describe only this specimen and do not capture a listener's expectation, phrase boundary, style, intention, emotion, or musical quality. Listener annotations remain separate lanes.

## Recording analysis

- **Schema:** `music-with-no-names.analysis.v1`.
- **Analysis version:** `mwno-audio-0.3.0`.
- **Inputs:** locally decoded mono samples, sample rate, channel count, and filename. Audio is never included in export.
- **Outputs:** 50 ms, 250 ms, and 1 s frames; RMS level; spectral-balance and band-energy proxies; flux and onset evidence; zero-crossing frequency; periodicity; harmonicity, salience, and roughness proxies; pulse candidates; onset phase; tempo envelope; syncopation; recurrence; and section candidates.
- **Confidence:** periodicity and pulse confidence are normalized internal model evidence. Roughness, harmonicity, and salience carry explicit evidence-confidence values derived from signal level, periodicity, brightness, and local change; these are model-internal confidence measures, not population-calibrated probabilities.
- **Known failures:** dense mixtures, noise, polyphony, strong transients, expressive tempo, and long files can undermine estimates. Listener corrections are exported separately from measurements.

## Atlas and personal-response models

- **Atlas schema:** `mwno-landmark.v1`.
- **Inputs:** declared measured values, curator hypotheses, uncertainty, listener spread, layer, provenance, and licensing.
- **Outputs:** porous descriptive placements and listener/goal-conditioned proximity—not quality rankings.
- **Live phrase bridge version:** `music-with-no-names.live-atlas.v1`. A valid tab-scoped phrase of at least four usable attacks yields relationship recurrence from pitch-class and relative-shape recurrence, piece-local surprise from incremental transition evidence, and timing activity from attack density plus onset-gap regularity. Missing evidence remains absent rather than being imputed. The square live mark and circular curator mark stay separate; changing the selected landmark never changes the live phrase values.
- **Personal terrain version:** `mwno-personal-1`; weighted centers and bandwidths are learned from local ratings with visible sample-count uncertainty.
- **Limits:** the bundled Atlas is a teaching set, not a statistically representative genre corpus. Its density clouds are derived only from the declared small starter corpora and must not be generalized to all music in a genre. The live bridge compares a short MIDI-only phrase with curator hypotheses about whole recordings and intentionally does not compute similarity, a nearest landmark, style, emotion, quality, or causal explanation. Timing activity is narrower than embodied drive. Personal models with few samples are unstable and remain on the device.
