# Research ledger

This ledger ties empirical claims to research anchors and marks where the product deliberately uses a simplified demonstrator rather than a validated estimator. It is not a claim that one paper settles a musical concept.

## Auditory frequency selectivity and roughness

- **Evidence anchor:** Plomp and Levelt connected judgments of simple-tone intervals to critical bandwidth and found the most dissonant region near a fraction of that bandwidth. DOI: [10.1121/1.1909741](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1909741).
- **Further auditory-filter evidence:** Glasberg, Moore, Patterson, and Nimmo-Smith measured asymmetric auditory-filter shapes and variation with listener age. DOI: [10.1121/1.391584](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.391584).
- **Product claim allowed:** Nearby partials can interact differently depending on absolute register and spectral content.
- **Product claim prohibited:** A roughness output determines dissonance, emotion, or goodness.
- **Implementation status:** Ear Lab uses a declared Plomp–Levelt/Sethares-style pair interaction and a critical-band-rate energy projection. It is an educational hypothesis, not a calibrated auditory periphery.

## Temporal attention, pulse, and expectation

- **Evidence anchor:** Large and Jones modeled attending rhythms that entrain to time-varying event structure and tested predictions in listening experiments. DOI: [10.1037/0033-295X.106.1.119](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.1.119).
- **Product claim allowed:** Pulse is a listener-facing hypothesis with confidence, not merely a written grid.
- **Product claim prohibited:** One inferred pulse proves meter or groove.
- **Implementation status:** Rhythm Lab exposes multiple pulse hypotheses, nested phase, microtiming, syncopation, tempo, and listener tapping. Recording Journey adds autocorrelation candidates, onset phase, a local tempo envelope, and syncopation evidence. These remain educational estimators rather than validated meter or groove models.

## Scale degree, tonal context, and movable coordinates

- **Evidence anchor:** Krumhansl and Kessler elicited context-dependent probe-tone profiles after scales, chords, and cadences in Western tonal materials, finding stable hierarchies within those listeners and stimuli. DOI: [10.1037/0033-295X.89.4.334](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.89.4.334).
- **Learning evidence:** Krumhansl and Keil found developmental differences in judgments of tonal function, supporting the treatment of tonal hierarchy as an acquired internal organization rather than a direct readout of frequency ratio. DOI: [10.3758/BF03197636](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03197636).
- **Product claim allowed:** A degree can acquire contextual stability or pull relative to a declared center, and that organization can be practiced with transposable relational labels.
- **Product claim prohibited:** A scale degree has a fixed tension, emotion, or goodness independent of sequence, style, listener, timbre, and register.
- **Context-effect evidence:** Bigand and Pineau experimentally varied global harmonic context around locally identical targets, demonstrating that perceived function depends on preceding organization. DOI: [10.3758/BF03205524](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03205524).
- **Pitch-imagery evidence:** Gelding, Thompson, and Johnson developed a tonal-context task in which listeners mentally traverse pitch steps before judging an audible probe; the task supported a range of musical backgrounds and required an explicit behavioral response. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0121809](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121809).
- **Learning-design anchor:** Roediger and Karpicke found stronger delayed retention after retrieval than restudy in their prose-learning experiments. The product borrows the prediction-before-reveal structure as a design hypothesis; it does not claim that this specific music lesson has been validated. DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x).
- **Implementation status:** Scale Lab v1.23 uses movable solfège as a culturally situated relational mnemonic, not a physical universal. It declares a 2:1 octave realized in twelve equal logarithmic slices, applies physically invariant small/medium/large gap labels, and keeps melodic distance separate from matched harmonic-spectrum interaction. Action-first copy asks learners to listen before opening measurements. Context A/Bs collect the learner’s report before interpretation. Scale-specific inner-hearing phrases require a degree choice and restore the answer in context. Piano Lab v1.35 then treats equal-tempered keys, conventional note names, scale names, staff notation, and the circle of fifths as coordinated learning overlays on top of frequency and interval relationships. Its live phrase ribbon retains sixty seconds of observed velocity, release, duration, pedal extension, and silence while the coordinated detail views magnify seven attacks; the scale frame uses that full phrase evidence so the microscope cannot silently redefine the context. Focused Intervals, Scales, Chords, Motion, Paths, and Experience lenses keep the same specimen visible. The Scales lens exposes the selected route first as a cyclic gap fingerprint, then separates compatibility from a duration-, recurrence-, attack-, low-register-, and ending-weighted tonal-gravity heuristic. Competing centers remain visible and are not detected keys. Silent resolution forks preserve contrasting center-return, hand-motion, fifths-neighbor, and recurrence intentions until the learner performs one. The Motion lens now separates measured finger contact, pedal extension, overlap, and silence from a modeled recurrence trail. Its motif detector compares exact semitone shapes and normalized onset gaps in short local windows to teach repetition, transposition, rhythmic variation, altered endings, and return; it does not infer written articulation, compositional intention, phrase boundaries, or listener salience. The Paths lens fixes the current movable Do and turns generated pop-loop, blues-cycle, classical-cadence, and pedal-point archetypes into silent, stable ghost-key sequences. Exact learner-performed pitch-class matches advance the sequence in any octave while carried tones, nearest-key motion, fifths travel, modeled texture, and tonal tendency remain separate. These are authored relationship examples with explicit provenance, not song transcriptions, style detectors, or universal genre claims. The Experience lens freezes a phrase, then asks the learner to report settledness, energy, surprise/familiarity, and liking before mapping those reports with sample-count uncertainty. Relationship signatures compare exact interval paths and normalized timing across transposition and uniform tempo changes; they do not infer that the performances felt alike. Chord timing boundaries are explicit and learner-correctable. Attacked chord membership remains distinct from inherited held/pedal context; incomplete chord outlines do not imply an exact root. Interval Echo checks transposed spacing without producing sound. Nearby chord suggestions mark silent ghost keys rather than entering an answer; the learner must perform the target. Completed chord gestures expose nearest-key held, rising, falling, added, and released strands plus parallel, contrary, and oblique classifications, which are not claims about fingering or voice identity. Causal chord copy reports changed pitch membership before separate roughness, tonal pull, and repose deltas. The selected sine, harmonic, mellow-piano, or bright-piano teaching spectrum is disclosed with a partial comb and does not analyze the connected instrument’s audio. Switching it changes only spectral proxies; keys, intervals, scale/fifths context, rhythm, tonal pull, novelty, and listener reports stay fixed. Chord labels, roughness, tonal pull, repose, pitch-set novelty, fifths travel, voice motion, performed center evidence, articulation timing, motif recurrence, landmark progress, measured phrase facts, modeled proxies, and listener reports remain separate. Its MIDI path remains silent and analysis-only. These are teaching hypotheses, not claims that one tuning, route, chord, grouping, target, continuation, motif, archetype, or perceptual response is natural or culturally universal.

- **Piano v1.36 extension:** the Scales focus makes the previously disclosed fifths derivation manipulable beside the live phrase. It repeats the exact 3:2 relationship, folds octaves, exposes the approximately 23.46-cent mismatch at the attempted twelfth-fifth return, and interpolates each fifth toward 700 cents so the displayed closure gap reaches zero. Radius separates move order and is not a frequency dimension; angle carries accumulated displacement from equal-key fifth positions. Selecting a fifths position changes the declared movable Do and coordinated labels without creating a note. This teaches the design tradeoff between ratio purity and cyclic keyboard closure; it does not claim that twelve-tone equal temperament, the circle metaphor, or movable-Do pedagogy is natural, universal, or aesthetically superior.

- **Piano v1.37 extension:** chord gestures now maintain three separate facts: which notes were attacked during the timing cluster, which notes were physically sounding at its close, and which of those notes the learner currently interprets as belonging to the chord. Inherited held or pedal tones begin in the interpretation but remain visibly distinct; each can be excluded or restored. Identity, tonal tendency, pitch-set novelty, exact-root travel, nearby targets, causal comparison, and nearest-key voice strands follow the corrected interpretation. The pairwise interval texture and spectrum-dependent roughness retain the complete sounding field. This is an inspectable learner correction, not proof of harmonic intention, a recovered score, or a claim that an excluded note is inaudible or musically irrelevant.

- **Piano v1.38 extension:** the Scales focus restores retrieval practice as a single live gap-walk question. Starting locks the current movable Do and declared route, but the learner’s first Do may be in any MIDI octave. Each correct attack advances by the route’s exact equal-key gap; a mismatch retains completed positions and compares the attempted move with the expected one instead of resetting the entire exercise. The next frequency and equal-tempered ratio, a nearby small-integer landmark, repeated-fifths coordinate, and changing performance-weighted center evidence are shown as different descriptions of the same route position. The upper Do demonstrates exact 2:1 doubling while the gap string stays invariant. This is a deterministic equal-tempered retrieval aid, not evidence about fingering, expressive scale performance, universal solfège categories, natural tuning, tonal certainty, musical quality, or learning gains without participants.

- **Piano v1.39 extension:** the Chords focus restores controlled sonority recipes as a live one-variable experiment. Four authored fields begin from exact equal-key offset sets, while a learner can adopt any active exact-register field. The controls only outline keys. After the baseline is performed, a single entered or released note exposes only its new or lost pairwise intervals before separate assumed-auditory, selected-context, and conditional-affordance consequences. The display suppresses those causal lanes when more than one note differs. Reload requires the exact stored baseline to be replayed because physically active MIDI keys are not browser state. This is a deterministic comparison under a disclosed spectrum and selected tonal frame, not an analysis of the connected instrument's audio, a combined consonance or quality score, an emotion prediction, a recommendation, or evidence that isolating a note explains its role in a larger musical sequence.

- **Piano v1.40 extension:** the Motion focus now presents Pulse, Touch, Voices, and Motif as mutually exclusive questions over one retained phrase. Pulse does not infer a beat from melody. The learner arms the experiment, chooses an anchor key through the first attack, and repeats that exact key four times. A bounded median gap becomes the declared time unit; later attacks within 70 ms of one another form one onset cluster before phase and gap-ratio comparison. Phase and ratio landmarks are mathematical coordinates, not score values or performance targets. Source-tap IDs persist so a reload preserves the same evidence, while a tap that ages out of sixty-second phrase memory expires the coordinate rather than allowing unrelated later notes to replace it. This is a transparent timing microscope, not meter recognition, transcription, groove or swing analysis, tempo-following, technique assessment, a timing score, or evidence of musical quality or learning gains.
- **Piano v1.41 extension:** the performed fingerprint exercise begins from a learner-chosen key and records only strictly ascending equal-key gaps until the exact octave closes. It then supports retrieval by transposition and cyclic rotation before offering an optional catalog translation. The invariant is the ordered or cyclic gap relationship, not an absolute note label, emotional character, cultural status, fingering, or quality. A catalog match says only that the authored equal-tempered route equals one stored teaching route; no match is not an error, and rotation alone does not establish a heard tonal center or mode.

- **Piano v1.42 extension:** the tonal-gravity microscope freezes a performed phrase and turns one declared contextual cue into an explicit intervention. Held time, recurrence, attack strength, low-register placement, or ending support is assigned to one sounded pitch class while route fit and the other four cue lanes remain numerically invariant. The paired twelve-center display therefore answers only how sensitive this teaching heuristic is to that lane. A hands-on replay prompt invites the learner to create new evidence, but the synthetic intervention itself does not edit sound, model cue interactions, detect a key, reproduce a listener's perception, or determine tension, preference, emotion, correctness, or quality.

- **Piano v1.43 extension:** the Explore focus now turns the same-phrase/five-lens organizing model into a learner-controlled A/B. A recent phrase is frozen, the tonal frame stays fixed, and the learner performs the replay. Absolute key center, span, MIDI attack, signed intervals, normalized onset shape, elapsed time, sounding overlap, leading center hypothesis, ending, and first-to-second model separation remain individually inspectable. Uniform transposition and proportional timing are represented as invariants rather than as identical performances. Settledness, energy, and liking are supplied only by the learner and are not derived from the other lanes. The comparison does not align unequal sequences, infer intention, analyze the DAW's audio, establish causation among deltas, measure musicality, or rank either performance as better.
- **Piano v1.44 extension:** the site now treats Piano as a persistent live companion rather than one destination in a flat list. Start, Piano, and Personal Lens remain at the top level; supporting labs are grouped by the kind of question they answer. Leaving Piano does not discard a valid tab-scoped phrase: a compact return cue exposes only attack count and the learner-selected focus lens, then restores the same session and relational frame. This is an information-architecture and continuity intervention, not evidence that the grouping improves learning, that one path suits every learner, or that attack count or lens choice measures musicality.
- **Piano v1.45 extension:** the whole-phrase A/B begins with a learner-declared intervention rather than an open-ended replay. Each intention names one target coordinate and one intended control condition before phrase B exists. After capture, the interface reports whether the target crossed its declared display threshold, whether the control remained intact, and which sound, relationship, motion, or modeled-context lenses also moved or remained invariant. This structure supports causal inquiry but does not establish causation: real performances change several uncontrolled variables, thresholds are teaching conventions, MIDI attack is not acoustic loudness, context remains model-dependent, and the learner's settledness, energy, and liking reports cannot be attributed to any one visible delta.
- **Piano v1.46 extension:** Interval practice now turns a selected two-note relationship into two assumed partial combs on one log-frequency coordinate. Solid circle links mark pairs within 18 cents; dashed diamond links mark at most six stronger non-aligned interaction zones above a declared normalized threshold. The adjacent readout keeps exact frequency ratio, alignment, beat-rate coordinate, aggregate roughness, and spectral overlap separate so a learner can see how one interval can preserve its key distance while the timbre assumption changes its upper-partial evidence. MIDI supplies fundamentals only; the display does not inspect the keyboard patch, DAW, room, loudness at the ear, or listener. The window, threshold, proxy functions, and link count are explanatory choices, not fitted perceptual boundaries or evidence of consonance, emotionality, preference, goodness, or learning gains.
- **Piano v1.47 extension:** Interval Echo now retains the ordered source pair and compares it with independent two-attack replays. When the unsigned key span matches, the equal-tempered ratio remains invariant under register change, while the displayed hand-center position, direction, absolute frequencies, hertz separation, and assumed-spectrum interaction can change. This makes transposition a controlled relationship experiment rather than a success badge. The visual does not assert that two intervals in different registers sound identical: critical-band roughness can vary, instrument spectra and loudness are not captured by MIDI, direction and context remain audible, and no result predicts fingering, listener response, correctness, musicality, preference, or quality.
- **Piano v1.48 extension:** the Explore view now answers “What did this attack change?” through one selected event and five provenance-labeled lenses. The causal boundary is intentionally strict: only an isolated newly entered MIDI member receives newly created interval attribution. Field releases or other entries between snapshots make the display observational, repeated attacks remain temporal events without new membership, and the oldest visible event receives no fabricated baseline. The Context lane remains relative to the selected movable-Do route, and the Experience lane stays empty until the listener reports. This is an explanatory event comparison, not evidence that the attack caused a modeled context change, that MIDI velocity equals loudness, that the selected note has one function, or that any lane predicts emotion, preference, correctness, musicality, goodness, or learning gains.

## Sonority, consonance, and emotional affordance

- **Evidence anchor:** Eerola and Lahdelma found that roughness, harmonicity/familiarity, and spectral-envelope descriptors contribute to consonance ratings across several chord datasets, while also documenting strong predictor collinearity and stimulus limitations. DOI: [10.1177/20592043211030471](https://doi.org/10.1177/20592043211030471).
- **Register evidence:** Lahdelma and Eerola found a curved relationship between chord register and consonance ratings, consistent with low-register roughness and high-register sharpness both contributing. DOI: [10.3758/s13423-021-02033-5](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-021-02033-5).
- **Unfamiliar-system evidence:** Smit and colleagues found that roughness, harmonicity, spectral entropy, mean pitch, and cultural-system dissimilarity all contributed to affect ratings of unfamiliar Bohlen–Pierce chords. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0218570](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0218570).
- **Cross-cultural guardrail:** Smit and colleagues found that major/minor happiness associations were strongest where listeners had exposure to Western-influenced music and negligible in the least-exposed group, while noting uncertainty about universality. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0269597](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0269597).
- **Product claim allowed:** A realized sonority can make sensory and structural qualities—such as friction, fusion, openness, brightness, ambiguity, or repose—more or less available under declared assumptions.
- **Product claim prohibited:** A ratio set contains a fixed emotion, predicts what a listener must feel, or has an intrinsic goodness value.
- **Implementation status:** Harmony Lab v1.17 links realized spectra to auditory evidence, structural readings, and four explicitly conditional affordances. The displayed weights are inspectable educational heuristics, not fitted emotion estimates. The map changes with register, voicing, amplitude, spectrum, and recent motion; listener, culture, sequence, rhythm, dynamics, lyrics, and purpose remain outside this static model.

## Anticipation and musical reward

- **Evidence anchor:** Salimpoor and colleagues reported distinct striatal involvement during anticipation and peak pleasurable experience in a selected listener/stimulus design. DOI: [10.1038/nn.2726](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2726).
- **Product claim allowed:** Anticipation and experienced payoff can be temporally distinct parts of a rewarding arc.
- **Product claim prohibited:** A feature lane predicts dopamine, universal emotion, or pleasure for an individual.
- **Implementation status:** Journey separates uncertainty before events, surprise after events, recurrence, and listener annotations. It does not infer neurochemistry.

## Model governance

- Every model has a version, visible inputs, and limitations.
- Curator placements in the Atlas are labeled as hypotheses.
- Listener reports remain separate from computed predictions.
- Research citations justify questions and model families, not universal musical laws.
- Known missing validation: calibrated loudness, a real auditory filterbank, inter-listener studies, hearing-impaired listener calibration, long-file performance, and reproduced published test conditions.
