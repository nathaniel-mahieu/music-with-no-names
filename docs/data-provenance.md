# Dataset and fixture provenance

## Bundled audio

No commercial or third-party recording is bundled. Recording Lab operates on audio the listener explicitly selects from their own device; that audio remains in browser memory and is not exported.

## Generated fixtures

- **Recurrence + rupture demo:** synthesized in the browser from 220 Hz plus a 330 Hz or 277 Hz component, gated at a 500 ms pulse. Authored for this project; no external rights are implicated.
- **Analytic sine fixtures:** generated at test time from closed-form sine functions at declared frequencies and sample rates.
- **Controlled Ear Lab fields:** synthesized at interaction time from declared fundamentals, partial amplitudes, inharmonic stretch, noise amount, attack, and duration.

## Open recording fixture candidate

`fixtures/open/manifest.json` pins Rubinkumar's fourteen-second *A Major Scale* recording from Wikimedia Commons under CC0 1.0, including its source page, direct media URL, declared byte count, and Wikimedia SHA-1 checksum. The deterministic fetch script rejects any size or checksum mismatch, and the test suite rechecks the bundled bytes. Browser codec coverage remains in the real-device matrix.

## Atlas teaching data

The initial Atlas entries are hand-authored pedagogical landmarks and transformations. They are not measurements of bundled copyrighted recordings and do not constitute a representative genre dataset. Each entry carries source type, method, version, layer, confidence or spread, and licensing metadata. The Atlas computes explicitly provisional density regions from three pop profiles, two blues profiles, and four selected-classical profiles; counts and methods remain visible, and the regions are deliberately porous and overlapping.

The v1.51 live bridge does not add a recording corpus. Its live square is derived only from the current tab's retained MIDI attacks; the selected landmark's circular marks remain the existing curator-authored, whole-recording hypotheses. No phrase, audio, similarity result, or landmark assignment is uploaded or persisted as new Atlas data.

The v1.52 Rhythm bridge derives its pitchless timeline only from that same tab-scoped MIDI phrase. It does not capture audio, fetch timing examples, add the phrase to a dataset, or upload attacks, velocity, or release times. Its ratio landmarks are authored mathematical reference coordinates, not learned genre norms.

The v1.53 Journey bridge derives field shape, bass motion, relative onset time, and continuation counts only from that tab-scoped MIDI phrase. It does not capture audio, query or add a corpus, identify a work or style, persist a learned population model, or upload the phrase. Its alternatives are counts inside the current specimen, not recommendations or crowd expectations.

The v1.54 Harmony bridge derives attacked chord fields from that same tab-scoped phrase plus the learner's stored chord-timing and break/join corrections. Its auditory deltas are generated from a fixed nine-partial exact-harmonic teaching spectrum rather than keyboard or DAW audio; MIDI velocity is not treated as loudness. It does not add a corpus, upload a phrase, reconstruct pedal-inherited chord membership, infer emotion, or store a listener model.

The v1.55 Ear bridge derives one interval from the latest two valid attacks in that same tab-scoped phrase. MIDI key numbers and event timing supply movement, onset spacing, and release-proven overlap; the app derives equal-tempered ratios and A4=440 12-TET reference frequencies. Neither source supplies acoustic audio or upper partials. Four generated spectra—sine, exact harmonic, mellow-piano proxy, and bright-piano proxy—are compared only after overlap is proven. No phrase or audio is uploaded, no keyboard or DAW spectrum is analyzed, MIDI velocity is not acoustic loudness, and no sensory proxy is stored as a listener response, preference, emotion, or quality label.

The v1.56 Interval Context Echo uses only two frozen tab-scoped MIDI events and one later two-attack replay. It derives key span, A4=440 12-TET reference frequencies, attack gap, direction, release-proven overlap, and the second attack's sounding MIDI field from those local events. Current movable-Do labels are a selected teaching overlay. The chosen teaching spectrum contributes roughness and partial overlap only when both pairs prove simultaneous overlap; no keyboard or DAW audio, listener response, or inferred musical function is added or uploaded.

The v1.57 Interval Experience Handoff copies those exact four local MIDI events into the existing Personal Character Map only after the learner selects `Reflect on source + echo`. The retained live phrase remains unchanged. Timing, key span, velocity, release, field membership, and assumed-spectrum summaries remain measured or modeled evidence; settledness, energy, familiarity, and liking are stored only after explicit listener answers. The resulting report stays in browser-local storage, enters the existing explicit JSON export and two-step deletion flow, and is never uploaded automatically.

The v1.58 Chord Change Experience Handoff copies the attacks belonging to the selected corrected chord gesture and its immediately preceding corrected gesture only after the learner selects `Reflect on chord change`. Boundary and inherited-membership corrections determine which two interpreted fields are compared, while the copied event records retain their original MIDI note, onset, velocity, release, and sounding-field evidence. The sixty-second live phrase is not shortened or replaced. Field size, key span, entered/left/stayed positions, nearest-key motion, and declared-spectrum or selected-Do teaching coordinates remain measured, interpreted, or modeled evidence; settledness, energy, familiarity, and liking remain absent until the listener answers. The bounded specimen persists through Piano focus changes, returns to whole-phrase mode only by explicit learner action or Clear, and follows the same browser-local export and deletion policy as other phrase reports.

The v1.59 Chord Voicing Echo freezes the selected corrected field as unique MIDI positions plus its performed source records. Those records include every attack in the grouped gesture and, when interpreted membership contains held or pedaled notes, the most recent earlier attacks that supplied those positions. The source, grouping anchor, selected Do, and scale route live only in tab-scoped session storage and remain available if the live sixty-second phrase later prunes the original events. Each later grouped gesture is an independent attempt. Pitch-class identity or one uniform transposition is an interpreted relationship result; register, frequencies, span, bass, doubling, nearest-key mapping, assumed-spectrum roughness, and selected-Do coordinates remain separate measured or modeled evidence. Only an explicit matched handoff copies source and latest revoicing records into local listener reflection. No MIDI, inferred relationship, or model output is uploaded or converted into emotion, preference, correctness, similarity, or goodness.

The v1.60 Chord Motion Echo freezes two selected corrected fields as unique MIDI positions and their exact grouped or inherited supplying attacks. The current attempt anchor is stored beside—but never substituted for—the source anchor. Only the first two later valid grouped fields belong to that attempt; `Try another move` advances the attempt boundary explicitly, preventing a later chord from being silently reused across comparisons. One shared pitch-class shift across both fields is an interpreted transformation result. Endpoint shifts, common/entered/left membership, register, frequency, physical voice mapping, bass movement, assumed-spectrum model paths, and selected-Do coordinates remain separate evidence. A matched reflection deduplicates shared inherited attacks and copies only the two source fields plus two replay fields while retaining the complete live phrase. Source, attempts, and reports stay browser-local and are never uploaded or converted into function, emotion, preference, similarity, correctness, or goodness.

The v1.61 Tonal Frame Echo adds no performed or persisted event. After a nonzero structural match, a component-local display choice either keeps the source Do for replay interpretation or offsets replay Do by the matched transposition. The latter recomputes only relative syllables and the existing selected-context teaching model; raw MIDI positions, frequencies, source and replay fields, assumed spectra, voice mapping, the relationship decision, and the bounded reflection specimen remain byte-for-byte unchanged. A retry makes the effective choice fixed-Do again through its new attempt anchor. The counterfactual is neither exported nor treated as a listener report, heard function, tonal certainty, emotion, preference, correctness, or goodness.

The v1.62 Octave Fold adds no new event, audio, corpus, or persisted learner state. It maps the already interpreted source and attempt MIDI fields to unique modulo-twelve positions, records how many physical octave doublings were removed, measures the adjacent gaps including octave closure, and rotates that cyclic sequence to its lexicographically smallest comparison form. That rotation is an algorithmic anchor only—not a detected root, Do, spelling, function, or listener percept. The reveal stays component-local and attempt-scoped. Physical MIDI, frequencies, register, bass, voice mapping, assumed-spectrum outputs, selected-context outputs, the structural match, and the bounded reflection specimen remain unchanged.

The v1.63 Gap Mutation adds no audio, corpus, genre prior, note-name dictionary, or persisted learner state. It compares the same interpreted source and attempt fields only when they have the same number of unique modulo-twelve positions, exactly one source-only position, exactly one attempt-only position, and at least two retained positions. Both loops begin at the numerically first retained pitch class only so their adjacent gaps can share an index; that deterministic anchor is not a root, movable-Do choice, voice correspondence, or perceptual claim. Signed deltas are derived from the two gap arrays, and both arrays must close at twelve. The existing physical events, five evidence lenses, sound-model outputs, selected frame, and listener reports remain unchanged.

The v1.64 Scale Landing Mutation adds no audio, corpus, genre prior, note-name dictionary, scale-name prior, or new persistent record. Its only inputs are the completed source gap array and a later completed free-play gap array; each contains positive integer equal-key steps that sum to twelve. Cumulative positions are derived deterministically from zero through twelve. Set differences identify retained, source-only, and attempt-only landings, while elementwise subtraction supplies signed gap deltas only when both routes have the same gap count. The physical MIDI origins, absolute frequencies, performed phrase, selected frame, assumed spectrum, listener reports, and optional conventional translations are not inputs to the comparison and are not changed by it.

The v1.65 Interval Ripple adds no external data and persists no new state. It accepts only a successful one-key Scale Landing Mutation. For each retained cumulative position, absolute integer distance to the source and new landing is converted to the exact twelve-equal multiplier `2^(distance/12)`. The changed-spoke count is the number of retained positions; the invariant control count is the deterministic number of unordered retained-position pairs. Source/new bar lengths are direct renderings of those distances. No note label, scale catalog, MIDI origin, absolute frequency, sound model, audio, tonal-center model, population response, or learner report enters the calculation.

The v1.66 Spectral Ripple persists one additional local exercise coordinate: the source route's first MIDI key. Once the learner selects a changed interval spoke, that source register is reused for both the source and one-key-mutated counterfactual, even when the performed mutation replay started elsewhere. MIDI-to-frequency conversion supplies only an A4=440 12-TET reference coordinate; it does not measure acoustic pitch. All partials, 18-cent alignment matches, strongest non-aligned interaction zones, aggregate roughness, and spectral overlap are regenerated from the already declared sine, harmonic, mellow-piano, or bright-piano teaching spectrum. No keyboard audio, DAW audio, microphone input, room response, listener response, scale name, tonal function, consonance label, emotion, preference, or quality value enters the calculation.

The v1.67 Ending Ripple adds no external data and persists no new specimen beyond the existing local phrase A/B session. Both performed MIDI note paths are normalized to their own first attack. The reveal is available only when the attack counts match, every earlier normalized position matches, and the normalized ending position differs. Each earlier attack then contributes one source/new signed interval to the ending; absolute interval size is converted to `2^(distance/12)`, and unordered earlier-to-earlier pairs supply the invariant count. The existing full-phrase gravity candidates may be displayed beside the physical ripple, but their duration, recurrence, velocity, bass, ending, and route evidence is neither recomputed from the ripple nor attributed solely to the final note. No recording, spectrum, corpus, cadence label, emotion model, preference inference, or quality score enters the relational calculation.

The v1.68 Chord Gesture Echo adds no audio, corpus, tempo grid, meter model, or listener label. It reuses the four performed chord gestures already held by Chord Motion Echo and records which source events were actual attacks rather than inherited sounding members. Each source and replay row is normalized to its own first attack. Field spread is the latest minus earliest onset; chord spacing is the difference between field anchors; MIDI attack means use transmitted velocity only. Overlap is proven by a known final release after the second-field anchor, while silence is reported only when every first-field final release is known; a key release without a final sounding release remains unknown under possible pedal extension. The progressive reveal is attempt-local and persists no derived score.

The v1.69 Resolution Landing Lens adds no audio, corpus, functional-harmony label, emotion model, or persistent derived score. Inputs are the existing local MIDI phrase, the armed fork’s source event ID and target pitch class, and its frozen movable-Do frame. The first later attack at that pitch class becomes the landing. Equal-tempered MIDI positions determine the signed source span, final approach, and frequency multiplier; onset timestamps determine both elapsed intervals; transmitted velocity supplies only the attack delta. The final predecessor’s recorded sounding release proves overlap, touching, or silence, while an unresolved final release stays unknown. A three-to-twelve-attack context can enter the existing local listener-report store only after an explicit reflection action.

The v1.70 Phrase Breath Map adds no audio, notation parser, phrase corpus, meter detector, emotion model, or persistent score. It reads only the existing local MIDI phrase’s pitch positions, attack times, and recorded sounding releases. Consecutive attacks within 70 milliseconds join one onset group, bounded to a 140-millisecond group span, so compact chords do not define the local timing unit. The median adjacent group gap supplies a phrase-local reference; the learner chooses a threshold from 1.2 to 3 times that reference. A candidate break appears only when the group gap crosses that threshold and every note in the preceding group has a recorded final release proving at least 120 milliseconds, or one quarter of the local reference, of silence. Unknown releases, touching releases, sounding overlap, pedal tails, and long held notes do not become quiet-space boundaries. The resulting timing islands are display-only grouping hypotheses, not detected phrases, breaths, meter, form, expression, correctness, or musical quality.

The v1.71 One-Pause Counterfactual adds no audio, score alignment, phrase corpus, expressive-performance label, emotion model, or persistent derived value. It compares the two learner-bounded specimens already captured by the whole-phrase timing experiment. The attack count and every absolute MIDI key must match. Each adjacent onset gap is compared against the source gap with a tolerance equal to the larger of 45 milliseconds or 12 percent; exactly one gap must cross its tolerance for the microscope to open. Source and replay sounding releases independently describe silence, touching, overlap, pedal-ended overlap, or unknown release evidence. The tolerance is an inspectable interface heuristic for ordinary human replay variation, not a fitted timing-error model, correctness threshold, phrase-boundary detector, or perceptual just-noticeable difference.

The v1.72 Motif Invariance Lens adds no audio, score, corpus, genre prior, notation name, listener label, or persistent derived value. It reuses one source/return pair from the existing local three- or four-attack motif detector. Each statement’s first MIDI position becomes zero; adjacent signed key differences form the pitch fingerprint. Each positive inter-onset gap is divided by that statement’s total onset span; these shares form the timing fingerprint and always sum to one. The existing detector’s mean absolute timing-share distance of 0.12 remains an inspectable interface heuristic rather than a perceptual threshold. The reveal explains the detector result only; it does not establish that the learner intended a motif, heard a return, created a formal function, expressed an emotion, played correctly, or produced musical quality.

The v1.73 Learner-Bounded Motif Echo adds one session-local source boundary but no audio, score, corpus, genre prior, listener label, or persistent derived score. The learner explicitly chooses the latest three or four MIDI attacks. The first equally sized sequence after that anchor is compared in full; no search, ranking, or shorter sub-window can replace it. Pitch paths, signed moves, and proportional onset gaps reuse the v1.72 normalization. Exact return, transposition, timing variation, altered ending, and multiple change are transparent rule labels under the same 0.12 mean timing-share heuristic. Retry advances only the replay anchor; releasing the source ends the exercise. A restored browser session retains the chosen source and boundary IDs, not a new musical or experiential judgment.

The v1.74 Motif Return Arc retains at most three completed learner-bounded replays in browser session state. It derives only their chronological rule labels: exact and transposed comparisons count as relationship returns, while timing, ending, and multi-property differences count as variations. The first return after the first variation closes the displayed local arc. No new source, corpus, listener data, audio, learned classifier, score, or perceptual threshold is introduced; older v1.73 sessions migrate with an empty replay history. The trace is not evidence of a section, theme, compositional intention, style, listener recognition, emotion, correctness, quality, or formal function.

The v1.75 Motif Experience Check adds one optional session-local listener report—`not-return`, `uncertain`, or `felt-return`—only after the latest open variation receives a rule-based relationship return. The report comes exclusively from a native button choice; it is never computed from MIDI, stored in a cohort, exported as a personality label, or used to retrain or recalibrate the detector. A newer kept variation clears the previous report and reopens the question. Earlier sessions migrate with no report. Modeled relationship recovery and listener experience may agree or disagree without becoming a recognition rate, form detector, emotion estimate, correctness judgment, or quality score.

The v1.76 Return Experience Contrast retains at most four local observations. Each contains the exact target event IDs, exact-versus-transposed rule label, signed starting-key shift, and one explicit return report. Re-reporting the same target replaces that observation; it does not add a vote. The display appears only after two observations and preserves their order without calculating agreement, counts by response, an average, recognition probability, listener profile, threshold, or generalization. v1.75's single compatibility field remains readable and becomes an event-addressed observation only when the learner makes the next report choice. No observation leaves browser session storage or enters the Personal Lens dataset.

The v1.77 Motif Experience Handoff derives one exact event-ID list from the currently retained source, the latest variation, and its first later exact or transposed relationship return. The interface resolves those IDs only against the live sixty-second phrase; it does not concatenate the separately normalized source and replay snapshots. If any required event has expired, the handoff is unavailable. A successful handoff freezes the original attacks, releases, velocities, fields, and chronology into the existing local Personal Character workflow. Only the learner's four explicit answers become a saved Personal Lens observation; the modeled return kind and prior return report do not fill, predict, or calibrate those answers.

The v1.78 Landmark Experience Handoff records the exact event IDs that supplied each matched field in one learner-performed generated path. Field order remains chronological, while a sustained common tone reused across adjacent fields remains one physical attack rather than becoming a synthetic duplicate. A complete capture must match the selected path, fixed center, route variant, and expected field count; malformed, legacy-incomplete, or aged-out IDs cannot open a reflection. The saved local observation may carry the generated path family, title, and original/transposed/one-key-changed label as context, but only the learner supplies settledness, energy, familiarity, and liking. No song recording, acoustic audio, style classifier, emotion label, correctness verdict, or goodness score is added.

The v1.79 Landmark Experience Contrast adds two optional bounded context fields to newly saved landmark reports: a generated path label and movable-center pitch class. Earlier v1.78 reports remain readable without either field. The comparison scans backward from the latest report until it finds one with an earlier report for the same path ID, then pairs it only with the nearest earlier matching report; it does not average, rank, resample, or search for a more dramatic contrast. The four listener ratings are copied verbatim. Key span and overlap come from each frozen MIDI specimen, while mean crunch and ending repose remain outputs of that report's declared teaching spectrum and tonal frame. Alignment does not establish that any measured or modeled difference caused a listener response, nor does two-report movement define a preference, emotion, style rule, goodness value, or learning effect.

The v1.80 Landmark Route Fingerprint adds no audio, corpus, score, listener data, or fitted model. It reads only the selected generated path's authored root offsets and pitch offsets. Each field is reduced modulo twelve relative to movable Do; the optional counterfactual replaces exactly its declared source offset with its declared target offset. Exact set intersection supplies carried positions. The existing stable close voicings at a fixed reference Do supply nearest-key total motion and largest leap, while the existing fifths coordinate supplies root travel. Because absolute center is intentionally absent, uniform transposition produces the same fingerprint; because register is folded away, the plot cannot describe a pianist's exact voicing or hand placement. It is not acoustic sound, detected tonal function, style membership, emotion, correctness, musical quality, or learning effectiveness.

The v1.81 Performed Route Comparison adds no new musical source. Route A and route B are selected from the same four curator-authored generated archetypes, and both must be completed by the learner before a comparison appears. The session stores only A's path ID and original-versus-one-key-changed structural variant plus B's path ID; it does not copy a performance or create a listener label. Exact normalized field-set intersection identifies shared field shapes. Unique-set counts supply recurrence, intersection across every field supplies through-tones, and the existing fingerprint transitions supply carried-position counts, stable-reference nearest-key motion, largest leap, and fifths travel. These sequences are displayed independently with no aggregate distance or similarity score. The comparison therefore concerns authored transposition-invariant route structure, not the learner's timing, articulation, register, DAW sound, tonal interpretation, emotion, familiarity, preference, style recognition, musical quality, or learning outcome.

The v1.82 Landmark Transition Change Lens adds no recording, corpus, listener data, or learned output. It selects exactly one adjacent move from the completed generated route. Pitch offsets are compared after the same movable-Do octave fold to identify stayed, entered, and left positions. The existing deterministic compact ghost-target sequence supplies physical MIDI keys and one nearest-key voice assignment; it is a stable teaching reference rather than a reconstruction of the learner's voicing or fingering. A4=440 12-TET reference frequencies derived from those target keys pass through the currently declared sine, harmonic, mellow-piano proxy, or bright-piano proxy to obtain before/after roughness and spectral-repose deltas. The existing selected-Do tonal-tendency heuristic supplies pull and home-evidence deltas, and the authored root offsets supply fifths travel. These evidence kinds remain labeled separately. No actual instrument pitch or spectrum, acoustic balance, timing, velocity, releases, pedal, intention, emotion, settledness, preference, correctness, musical quality, or learning effect is observed or inferred.

The v1.83 Shared Cycle Lens adds no recording, corpus, listener data, learned output, or audio capture. It converts two to six distinct performed MIDI positions to A4=440 12-TET reference frequencies. Candidate common periods are seeded from each reference frequency divided by harmonic numbers 1 through 16, fitted in logarithmic frequency, reduced by the harmonic-number greatest common divisor, and filtered to a non-dominated set over maximum harmonic number and RMS cents mismatch. Candidates with any mismatch above 35 cents are refused. Displayed waveform phases are reset together only to make closure drift visible; they are not observed performance phases. The lens does not inspect acoustic pitch, pitch bend, instrument tuning, or the keyboard's or DAW's upper partials, amplitude, envelope, room, hearing, root, chord identity, tonal function, consonance, emotion, preference, correctness, musical quality, or learning effect.

The v1.84 provenance pass adds no new event, model, score, corpus, persistence, or network activity. It makes an existing conversion boundary explicit throughout Piano and the live Ear bridge: Web MIDI supplies key numbers, note-on/off timing, velocity values, sustain state, and device metadata; the app maps key number 69 to A4=440 Hz and derives every displayed reference frequency by `440 × 2^((key−69)/12)`. Pitch-bend messages, instrument or DAW tuning, microphone/audio pitch, and acoustic spectra are not read. Therefore displayed hertz values are 12-TET teaching coordinates, not measured fundamentals of the connected instrument.

The v1.85 Resonance Sky adds no audio, recording, upload, corpus, learned output, listener inference, or new persistence category. Its positions, wakes, membranes, and filaments are deterministic projections of the same tab-scoped MIDI attacks, releases, pedal state, selected Do/scale, chord-window corrections, and selected assumed teaching spectrum. Angle is a fixed repeated-fifths display orientation; radius is derived from MIDI-key/log-reference-frequency register and clamps only at the extreme display edge; hue is a movable-Do relationship code. Attack size uses MIDI velocity, not measured loudness. A broken wake requires all earlier retained attacks to have known releases before the next onset and the next field to contain no inherited sounding position. Chord mist comes from the declared grouping window. Coral crunch is shown only when its source measure exactly matches the drawn note field. Interval dash density shows equal-key mismatch to a named interval reference; it neither ranks harmonic relatedness nor treats the geometric √2 octave midpoint as an integer ratio. The bounded twenty-eight-mark trail, register-spanning eight-note sample, and evenly sampled twelve-link cap are display budgets, not claims about musical salience; total and omitted pair counts remain in text.

## Listener data

Ear observations, Journey responses, and Personal Lens ratings are created by the current listener and stored only in that browser. No cohort data, identity, account, or backend aggregation exists. Export occurs only through an explicit local JSON action.
